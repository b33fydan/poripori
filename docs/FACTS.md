## The Arecibo Message

The bits, their layout and the meaning of each section: `data/arecibo/SOURCES.md`.

- **Pluto among the planets:** the message's Solar System section shows the Sun and nine planets, Pluto included; it was sent in 1974. The Solar System B-roll flies them in the message's order from the outermost in. Planet sizes and colours in that B-roll are stylized, not data.

## The Arecibo telescope

- **Where it stood:** 18°20′39″ N, 66°45′10″ W (18.3442° N, 66.7528° W), in Barrio Esperanza, Arecibo, Puerto Rico.
  - Wikipedia, "Arecibo Telescope": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arecibo_Telescope (read 2026-10-03). Gives the coordinates and "Esperanza, Arecibo, Puerto Rico".
  - US National Science Foundation, solicitation NSF 17-538, "Management and Operations of the Arecibo Observatory": https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/management-operations-arecibo-observatory/505401/nsf17-538/solicitation (read 2026-10-03). Describes "the 305-meter diameter, fixed, spherical reflector, located on approximately 120 acres of U.S. Government-owned land in Barrio Esperanza, Arecibo, Puerto Rico, some 10 miles inland from the city of Arecibo."
  - The NAIC's own observer's guide gives 18° 20′ 36.6″ N, 66° 45′ 11.1″ W (quoted in a search result; the page itself, www2.naic.edu, no longer resolves). That agrees with the above to within 0.001°.
  - On the voxel Puerto Rico (`data/earth/puerto-rico-mask.json`) these coordinates fall on land, 7 cells (about 13 km) south of the north coast, matching "some 10 miles inland".
- **Its shape:** a 304.8 m (1,000 ft) fixed spherical dish in the ground, with a platform suspended 150 m above it by 18 main cables from three reinforced concrete towers, one 111 m tall and two 81 m tall (Wikipedia, as above). The voxel telescope keeps that arrangement (a bowl, three towers, the tallest one taller, a platform hung on cables with a dome beneath) but not its proportions: it is drawn about 150 times too big for the island so it can be seen.
- **The island's outline:** Natural Earth 1:10m land polygons (public domain): `data/earth/SOURCES.md`.

## Not shown, for later

- The message was transmitted in 1974 toward the globular cluster Messier 13 (Wikipedia, as above). The beam in the film is a picture of sending it, not its real direction: the dish was fixed, pointing near the zenith.
- The telescope collapsed on 1 December 2020 (Wikipedia, as above).
